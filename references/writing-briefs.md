# Writing briefs

Define what belongs before drafting. Use the task context to fill this in privately; a simple reply does not need a questionnaire or a visible planning block.

```text
Job: what this piece must accomplish
Reader: who will use it, and what they already know
Deliver: artifact and useful shape
Keep: facts, conditions, voice, or relationship work that must survive
Leave out: content that does not belong in this particular output
```

A reusable role name can point to a saved brief. It does not establish expertise, invent the writer’s personality, or override the current request. Apply exclusions to this output, not every future message. Keep a reusable brief in the writer’s profile only when the writer asks to retain it or approves the recurring choice.

## Examples of scoped briefs

| Job | Useful output | Exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| Action-only team handoff, when requested | Work, supplied owner, supplied date, blocker or dependency, and next action | Background narrative, retrospective diagnosis, persuasive rationale |
| Decision brief | Recommendation, decisive evidence, tradeoff, and decision needed | Full chronology and options that cannot affect the choice |
| Personal reply | The requested human response in the established relationship | Status-report formatting, unsolicited solutions, invented feelings |

For an action-only handoff, a blocking condition still belongs: “Release after sign-off” is part of the action. Do not omit it as rationale. Missing owners or dates remain unknown; never fill them from the role label. If the user asks why, supply the explanation instead of enforcing a previous action-only brief.

## Length and revision

An output shape such as two sentences or three action bullets can make the request concrete. Treat it as a constraint to check, not a guarantee that generation will comply. Honor the actual requested limit; do not automatically lower it by 20 percent.

Draft for the brief, then make the one final editorial review in [writing workflows](writing-workflows.md). For a hard word or character limit, count the finished artifact with code or the destination’s counter, revise if necessary, and recheck required content. State the counting convention when it matters. Tokens, words, and characters are different units; a token ceiling can truncate an answer and is not an editing method. If required meaning cannot fit, state the conflict rather than silently dropping it.

The writer owns the core message and judgment. Once those are supplied or approved, the assistant can adapt their expression to another audience or format without changing the claim. Do not turn a named persona or a style label into evidence that this judgment has already been established.
